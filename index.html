THE CORE PROBLEM STATEMENT

Two Critical Challenges. One Integrated Solution.

Tackling aquatic weed ecological hazards and agricultural plastic-water waste through a unified circular innovation.

01
Aquatic Ecological Hazard

Invasive Water Hyacinth causes environmental problems and is largely underutilized.

Rapid, unchecked infestation of invasive water hyacinth (Eichhornia crassipes) chokes inland rivers and agricultural water reservoirs, disrupts freshwater ecosystems, depletes dissolved oxygen, and requires expensive manual harvesting. Despite containing high-tensile lignocellulosic fibers, millions of tons of harvested biomass are discarded on riverbanks with virtually zero value-added utilization.

TARGET Biomass valorization & nanocellulose extraction
02
Soil & Water Degradation

Plastic mulch waste and inefficient irrigation lead to soil pollution and unnecessary water consumption.

Conventional non-biodegradable polyethylene (PE) agricultural mulch films fragment into hazardous microplastics that permanently contaminate arable topsoil and impede root respiration. Simultaneously, schedule-based flood irrigation results in catastrophic evaporative loss and water runoff without real-time root-zone monitoring.

TARGET Biodegradable mulch film & ESP32 precision irrigation

TECHNICAL TIMELINE

How AgriLoop Works

A comprehensive six-phase methodology transitioning invasive weed biomass from aquatic extraction into intelligent field cultivation.

01
COLLECT Phase 1: Raw Feedstock

Water Hyacinth Biomass Harvesting

Collection of invasive water hyacinth (*Eichhornia crassipes*) petioles and stems from infested community water bodies. Thorough washing to eliminate silt, mechanical chopping, and solar drying to prepare uniform lignocellulosic precursor.

02
EXTRACT Phase 2: Cellulose Fractionation

Cellulose and Nanocellulose Processing

Subjecting dry powdered biomass to standardized alkaline pretreatment (dilute NaOH) to solubilize non-cellulosic hemicellulose and lignin, followed by green chlorine-free hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) bleaching. Controlled acid hydrolysis extracts high crystalline nanocellulose fibrils.

03
FORMULATE Phase 3: Material Synthesis

Biodegradable Mulch-Film Development

Compounding nanocellulose with biobased polymers (chitosan matrix) and eco-friendly plasticizers (glycerol). Addition of non-toxic crosslinkers (citric acid) and beeswax emulsion creates a durable, water-resistant mulch film cast via solvent evaporation.

04
DEPLOY Phase 4: Field Installation

Apply Mulch Film to Crop Beds

Laying the biodegradable film onto prepared crop ridges with drip lines running underneath. Cutouts facilitate direct seedling emergence while the film suppresses weed germination, stabilizes soil thermal gradients, and curtails evaporation.

05
SENSE Phase 5: Telemetry Sensing

Monitor Soil Moisture and Environmental Conditions

Capacitive dielectric soil sensors and microclimate probes (DHT22 / DS18B20) continuously read volumetric water content (VWC), soil temperature, and ambient relative humidity directly at root-zone depths.

06
AUTOMATE Phase 6: Edge Actuation

ESP32-Controlled Precision Irrigation

The onboard ESP32 evaluates telemetry against crop-specific moisture thresholds. Upon detecting water depletion, an optocoupler relay actuates low-power drip solenoids/pumps, replenishing hydration until target moisture equilibrium is re-established.

MATERIALS SCIENCE & SYNTHESIS

FROM BIOMASS TO BIO-MATERIAL

Engineering high-tensile, soil-degradable agricultural mulch film utilizing water hyacinth nanocellulose reinforcement.

TARGET FUNCTIONAL PERIOD
90–120 DAYS*
*Target value; requires laboratory and field validation.

Conversion Pipeline

Pretreatment dissolves non-cellulosic impurities to isolate cellulose fibers, followed by mechanical or chemical shearing to obtain nanocellulose fibrils. The biocomposite is compounded with biodegradable plasticizers and natural hydrophobic barriers.

Water Hyacinth Biomass → Pretreatment → Cellulose → Nanocellulose → Film Formulation → Biodegradable Mulch Film
Soil Mineralization Target

Unlike synthetic polyethylene, the nanocellulose film undergoes natural microbial assimilation post-harvest, converting into benign organic humus, water, and CO₂ without leaving hazardous microplastic residues.

Existing IoT hardware and material-processing approaches can support prototype development. Proven chemical delignification and solvent casting methods provide established benchmarks for lab extraction.

SCALABILITY

ENGINEERING MITIGATION

CHALLENGES & SOLUTIONS

Anticipating biological heterogeneity and environmental stressors through systematic engineering strategies.

CHALLENGE 01
Biomass Variation
Moisture, age, and chemical composition vary substantially across water bodies.
→
ENGINEERED MITIGATION
Standardized Pretreatment
Rigorous sorting, standardized drying to <10% moisture, and controlled alkali delignification protocols.
CHALLENGE 02
Film Performance
Pure cellulose films can exhibit excessive water sensitivity and low wet tensile strength.
→
ENGINEERED MITIGATION
Formulation & Thickness Optimization
Crosslinking with citric acid, chitosan matrix reinforcement, and hydrophobic beeswax micro-emulsion.
CHALLENGE 03
Controlled Biodegradation
Risk of prematurely decaying prior to crop harvest in warm, wet soils.
→
ENGINEERED MITIGATION
Composition & Thickness Tuning
Calibrating nanocellulose loading and crosslinker density to achieve targeted 90–120 day functional window.
CHALLENGE 04
Sensor Reliability
Soil salinity drift, mineral encrustation, and dielectric variability across soil textures.
→
ENGINEERED MITIGATION
Calibration & Periodic Validation
Corrosion-proof capacitive dielectric design with soil-specific calibration curves and temperature compensation.
